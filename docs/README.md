# OctoAcme Project Management Documentation

## Welcome

This folder is the central hub for OctoAcme's project management knowledge. It brings together the key practices, roles, and artifacts used to plan, execute, communicate, and improve work across cross-functional projects.

## Project Management Process Summary

OctoAcme follows a customer-first, iterative delivery model grounded in clear ownership, data-informed decisions, and psychological safety. The project lifecycle is structured around five phases: Initiation, Planning, Execution, Release, and Close & Retrospective. During Initiation, teams confirm the business need, define success metrics, and align stakeholders before moving into planning. Planning turns that approved direction into a prioritized backlog, milestone plan, and definition of done so work can be executed with clarity. Execution focuses on daily coordination, tracking progress, identifying blockers, and maintaining transparency through standups, reviews, and project boards. Release and deployment ensure features are verified, communicated, and deployed with rollback or mitigation plans in place. Finally, the retrospective phase captures lessons learned, closes the loop on action items, and supports continuous improvement.

OctoAcme also defines a clear operating model for roles and responsibilities. Product Managers define outcomes and priorities, Project Managers coordinate schedule, risks, and communication, Developers build and validate the solution, QA/Testing validates quality and acceptance criteria, and Stakeholders provide input, feedback, and approvals. Communication is treated as a first-class practice: weekly syncs, standups, stakeholder updates, and issue escalations help teams stay aligned and surface risks early. Risk management relies on a simple risk register and escalation paths so problems are addressed before they become blockers. Quality is reinforced through acceptance criteria, CI checks, testing requirements, security scans, and release readiness reviews.
